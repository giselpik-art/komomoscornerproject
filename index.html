In the modern era, business and marketing are thriving global industries that underpin almost every sector, from fashion and mining to agriculture and fisheries. The ability to sell effectively is a primary priority, as persuasive marketing directly translates into increased sales and improved economic status. Mastering these competencies provides a significant advantage to students and professionals alike. The soft skills gathered from these industries—such as negotiation, consumer psychology, and strategic communication—are highly adaptable and serve as the foundational tools necessary to achieve goals in any professional field.

Our IL project prioritizes the development of these marketing skills, requiring students to deeply understand the mechanics of their own business models. Throughout the process, market research was conducted to inform our initial branding, including the selection of our business name, logo, and overall aesthetic. As the project neared its conclusion, the focus shifted toward executing marketing strategies, establishing strategic pricing, and identifying target consumers. This phase of the project was driven by thorough research and planning to ensure our goals were met.

The name "Komomo’s Corner" was chosen specifically to evoke an atmosphere of warmth and cozy hospitality. We wanted our booth to stand out as a comfortable "nook" where customers felt relaxed rather than rushed. To visually manifest this brand identity, a color palette of purples and blues was selected for the booth decorations. Based on color theory, purple is associated with creativity and fairytale-like, which gives our brand a unique feel. Meanwhile, blue is utilized to foster a sense of calm, stability, and trust. This specific aesthetic was designed to differentiate our booth from competitors and create a welcoming "anchor" in a busy, loud market environment.

During the execution phase, an extensive and inventory was prepared. Our menu consisted of "brookies" (a brownie-cookie hybrid), savory tacos, homemade frozen yogurt, and refreshing beverages like milk tea and milk coffee. For customers looking for non-food items, we also produced slime, scented candles, acrylic keychains, and handmade crochet dolls. However, several technical hurdles were encountered during the production process that required immediate problem-solving. Specifically, the sugar content of the brookies was frequently adjusted to ensure the flavor profile was not too sweet, and the seasoning for the tacos was perfected only after multiple trial runs to get the spice levels just right.

Additionally, the physical products required a lot of trial and error. The chemical consistency of the slime was modified multiple times to reach a professional, non-sticky standard, and the fermentation of the yogurt was carefully monitored to avoid spoilage and ensure a smooth texture. Beyond product development, the project tested our internal organizational skills. It was noted that progress was occasionally hindered by a dependency on only a few specific members, which led to some miscommunication and minor disputes during stressful moments. To resolve these inefficiencies, responsibilities were redistributed more fairly to ensure every team member could contribute their talents. Ultimately, this project demonstrated that while high-quality products are essential, the success of a business is rooted in clear communication and the ability of a team to function as a unified, strategic entity. We learned that a good business isn't just about selling a product; it’s about creating an experience that people want to be a part of.

Globalization isn’t something that we can control, especially with more technologies and how connections all over the world are made easy with social media. In Indonesia, globalization has become something common where more and more economies, culture and making sure that Indonesia stays updated, this activity can be, and information is integrated worldwide. Though this may lead to discovering new technologies that lead to the loss of Indonesia’s culture due to dominant cultures that will overshadow local cultures. Indonesia is known as a country to have a lot of amazing and diverse culture. This is one of the reasons why our Integrated Learning Project was in the theme of folklores from Indonesia. Indonesia’s culture is preserved through this project that creates products from traditional batik fabric that also showcases the typical culture of the area where each group’s folklore originated. The products themselves can be in the shape of any sewing products: a bag, a ribbon, or a keychain. The products which can be used in our daily life can also remind customers of our culture and to never forget how beautiful our traditions and local products are.

Our Integrated Learning bazar Project was given by our school for us to learn more on different subjects through real experiences. On our Integrated Learning Bazaar Project, our group had the most difficulties on creating sewing products out of Batik. It was challenging for us to find the right product that we can use for everyday life and also a product that doesn’t take too long to make. A lot of discussion on this matter was held in our group before ribbons were chosen as our sewing product. The sewing itself also took a while, especially with the quantity we had to prepare for the pre-orders and also for us to sell at our bazar. Other than our sewing products, we also had to plan for our other products which are the biotechnology products and also the eco-friendly products tasked by our science teachers. One of the most challenging products to make other than the sewing products was the biotechnology where we had to find a product that our customers would like to buy. Survey forms in the form of Google forms were sent to our family and friends by our group to find out which products would be the best for our bazar. Frozen yoghurt ended up being chosen by us for our biotechnology product based on the survey results. Frozen yoghurt is known as one of the trendiest ways to eat a yoghurt. It was quite challenging for the biotechnology since we had to do a trial test before that where we tried fermenting the milk overnight to make the perfect yoghurt. It also took a while for me to figure out a way to make the yoghurt texture the best it could be. Though making the perfect yoghurt was done, we also had to figure out how to make it into an ice cream. We did a few trials on making it into an ice cream which failed a few times. The first try failed since the ice cream only lasted a while and it turned to an ice when we put it on the freezer, the second time also failed due to the texture of the ice cream that was frozen solid, meanwhile the third time which was a day before the bazar, We finally succeeded on making the perfect ice cream. The frozen yoghurt was described as creamy and sweet, with the fresh taste of homemade Greek yoghurt. The frozen yoghurt was mostly sold out during our bazaar while some didn’t since there was an error on our part that caused three frozen yoghurts not being sold. The frozen yoghurt was said to be very delicious by most of our customers including our biology teacher.

This Integrated Learning Project was very tiring and consists of a considerable amount of challenges. Though, in this project we were able to learn a lot of moral values that can be applied to my daily life from knowing more about other people’s personalities, never giving up, teamwork, learning more about Indonesia’s culture to learning more about eco-friendly and biotechnology products. Each part of this project was filled with challenges. We found out that teamwork is a very important aspect that needs to be fulfilled well in order to achieve the best results on this project.

Overall, this Integrated Learning Project has given us an amazing real-life experience on many things related to the material we learn at school. This project also taught us many important things that we can use in our daily lives.
